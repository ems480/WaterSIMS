RAPID SAND FILTER · BACKWASH GUIDE

THE CYCLE

1 · FILTRATION

Water flows down through 0.5–1.0 m of sand at 2–12 m/h. Floc is captured in the top layers; headloss climbs toward the 2.4 m terminal setpoint while effluent stays below 0.30 NTU.

2 · DRAIN-DOWN

Influent and effluent valves close; the water level drops to just above the media so air scour can agitate the bed without blowing water into the troughs.

3 · AIR SCOUR

≈50–60 m/h of air from the underdrain nozzles shakes grains against each other, shearing off attached floc and breaking up surface crust before the water wash.

4 · BACKWASH

Upflow at 36–50 m/h fluidizes the bed. Target 20–35% expansion: enough for grain collisions, not so much that media carries over the trough lips. Dirty water spills to the waste gullet.

5 · SETTLE

Wash valve closes; grains fall back — coarse first — leaving a graded, stratified bed with fines on top, ready to filter again.

6 · FILTER-TO-WASTE

The first minutes of filtrate are ripening water and go to waste until effluent drops below 0.10 NTU. Then the unit returns to service.

THE PHYSICS (COMPUTED LIVE)

Minimum fluidization from Wen & Yu; expanded porosity from Richardson–Zaki with terminal settling by Ferguson–Church; clean-bed headloss by Kozeny–Carman. Cold water is more viscous → lower vmf and more expansion at the same wash rate. Watch the fluidized headloss plateau near the buoyant weight of the bed (~0.67 m) — it stops rising once the bed lifts.

Remf = √(33.7² + 0.0408·Ga) − 33.7  ·  εe = (v/vt)1/n  ·  Expansion = (1−ε₀)/(1−εe) − 1

At your current recipe: vmf = 9.9 m/h expansion ≈ 22% h₀ = 0.27 m

Failure modes to explore: wash below vmf (bed never lifts → mudballs, cleanliness falls); overwash a deep bed (expanded surface above trough lips → media loss); short washes leave residual solids that raise baseline headloss and effluent.
